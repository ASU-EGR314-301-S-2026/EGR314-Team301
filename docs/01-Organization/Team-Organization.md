## Team Charter

Our team of ASU student engineers is committed to collaboration, creativity, and the application of technical knowledge. Using GitHub, electrical components, and hands-on system design, we will deliver a functional portfolio-worthy product that reflects our engineering growth. We strive to make an affordable and highly useful product that represents our knowledge and talents.

Our team created this charter to define our shared values and goals for delivering a creative, demonstrable, and semi-functional product.

---

## Product Mission Statement

Our team is committed to designing and implementing an affordable and accessible exploration rover for residential and commercial use that emphasizes reliability, modularity, and structured system design.

---

## Team Structure

Our team is organized around subsystem ownership to ensure clear responsibility and efficient progress throughout the project lifecycle. Each member is responsible for a primary module within the rover system, including control, drive, sensing, and imaging. This structure allows individual members to focus deeply on their assigned subsystem while still collaborating closely during integration and testing phases. By clearly defining ownership early in the project, we reduce overlap, improve accountability, and simplify troubleshooting when issues arise.

This structure was intentionally chosen over a purely hardware or software split because the rover architecture is modular by design. Assigning ownership by subsystem ensures that each portion of the system is developed holistically, from schematic design through firmware and validation. Shared ownership of the drive system reflects its complexity and the need for coordinated mechanical and electrical development.

## Roles and Responsibilities

Each team member maintains ownership of their respective hardware and software components, including schematic design, firmware development, validation testing, and documentation. In addition to subsystem ownership, responsibilities are divided to support project management tasks such as documentation updates, version control oversight, integration scheduling, and presentation preparation. During demonstrations and reviews, each member is prepared to explain both their specific module and how it interfaces with the overall system architecture.

## Communication Strategy

The team maintains consistent communication through scheduled meetings and structured digital collaboration. Weekly in-person meetings are used to review progress, discuss blockers, and define integration milestones. Between meetings, GitHub is used for version control, issue tracking, and documentation updates to ensure transparency and accountability across subsystems. Shared messaging platforms allow rapid technical discussion and coordination when debugging or integrating modules.

Technical decisions are made collaboratively and evaluated based on feasibility, system impact, and alignment with defined project requirements. This structured communication and tracking process helps prevent integration conflicts and ensures all members remain aligned on system goals.

---

## Development Workflow and Integration

Hardware and software development are performed in parallel within each subsystem before moving into staged integration checkpoints. Individual modules are first validated independently to confirm functionality and performance against defined requirements. Once validated, modules are integrated incrementally into the full rover architecture according to planned milestones.

This stepwise integration process reduces system-level risk and allows issues to be isolated efficiently. By validating components before full integration and documenting results through GitHub repositories and testing notes, the team improves overall system reliability and maintains a clear path toward final demonstration readiness.
